Edge ordering study / 24 Nov 2026

Does edge order improve graph reasoning?

Across 19 model configurations, GraphDO breadth-first ordering produced 66 exact answers out of 114. A layout designed to keep fewer vertices active produced 64 out of 114 and cost slightly more.

Coverage and unavailable configurations

Of 300 planned new slots, 216 yielded eligible responses. The collector sent 221 unique requests. Three were rejected and two returned responses above the requested completion limit. How we ran the study

Both layouts used the same edges, canonical node IDs and question text. We selected one public Hard v1 graph for each combination of task and size. The tasks were triangle counting, weighted shortest path and exact-length walk counting, at 32 and 64 vertices.

Starting from GraphDO’s breadth-first edge order, we swapped rows to reduce the peak number of vertices with incident edges both before and after a row boundary. We requested a 16,384-token completion limit for each call. A returned cutoff counts as an incorrect response. Rejected calls and responses above the requested limit are excluded.
