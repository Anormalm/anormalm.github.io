## 3. What is OSINT?

OSINT means finding things out from sources anyone can access, such as photos, maps, news, records and archives. Public doesn’t mean easy to find or easy to interpret; you still have to work out which sources answer your question. It also includes offline public material, though I’m focusing on the web today.

Sources: Bellingcat Online Investigation Toolkit — https://bellingcat.gitbook.io/toolkit

## 8. Those were two kinds of task

Those examples cover identifying an artwork and geolocating a photo. Other tasks include timing footage, checking its claimed context, tracing entities across sites and reconstructing events. OSINT also involves collecting and preserving material, checking reliability and analysing what it means; the Berkeley Protocol describes those parts of an investigation.

Sources: Bellingcat Toolkit — https://bellingcat.gitbook.io/toolkit; OSINT Framework — https://osintframework.com/; Berkeley Protocol · investigation process — https://humanrights.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Berkeley-Protocol.pdf

## 9. Where it is used

Storyful verifies footage by checking it against maps and street-level views. Amnesty’s digital verification and remote sensing work supports human rights investigations. Bellingcat reconstructed movements in the MH17 investigation from public photos and video. These are applications by practitioners, rather than results from an agent experiment.

Sources: Storyful geolocation — https://storyful.com/news/verified-by-storyful/inside-storyful-verification-workflow-geolocation/; Amnesty Evidence Lab — https://citizenevidence.org/; Bellingcat MH17 report — https://www.bellingcat.com/news/europe/2015/10/08/mh17-the-open-source-evidence/

## 10. Geolocation is already being tested

Bellingcat tested 20 models on 25 unpublished photos in 2025; every model made some wrong guesses. Anthropic’s 2026 evaluation also tests photo geolocation, using images without search tools, while its text-geolocation test allows constrained search. I see these as reasons to evaluate particular capabilities carefully. Our pilot covers a much smaller piece: matching and extracting information from prepared digests.

Sources: Bellingcat · Have LLMs Finally Mastered Geolocation? — https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/how-tos/2025/06/06/have-llms-finally-mastered-geolocation/; Anthropic · capability evaluations — https://www.anthropic.com/research/intelligence-targeting-conventional-weapons-capabilities

## 11. Where entity matching fits

Matching a painting or ship to a record is one operation inside some OSINT tasks. The broader investigation still involves collecting and preserving material, checking sources and analysing the evidence. I’m borrowing the iterative search pattern for that narrow part, and the pilot tests an even more restricted version using prepared digests.

Sources: Berkeley Protocol · investigation process — https://humanrights.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Berkeley-Protocol.pdf

## 12. The entity-matching agent

Your released agent addresses entity matching: it uses retrieval to connect an entity to an English Wikipedia title. The model can read a result and choose follow-up queries until it stops or reaches the cap, and a later stage extracts the title. That search loop is the part I adapted, rather than a complete investigation system. I used the release pinned at commit 9a63c316.

Sources: Released implementation · 9a63c316 — https://github.com/neulab/think-before-you-link/tree/9a63c31673d404f5107307539970bdebbea595ca

## 13. What I ran

I kept the upstream _layer1_agentic_once tool-response loop and ran Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct with search caps of 1, 4 and 12. Retrieval used SQLite FTS5 over 22 prepared source digests. I adapted the prompt, retrieval and structured output, and omitted the original Wikipedia scoring.

Sources: Pinned upstream implementation — https://github.com/neulab/think-before-you-link/tree/9a63c31673d404f5107307539970bdebbea595ca; Pilot setup and results — pilot-results.txt
